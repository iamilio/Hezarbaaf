# Buildability rules and where they come from

Hezarbaaf checks every wall against these rules. Each one was checked against a published source in November 2026.
The checks are a designer's aid, not a structural design: tall, loaded or exposed walls still need an engineer.

| Rule | Value in Hezarbaaf | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Step from one course to the next (corbel) | ≤ smaller of ½ nominal height and ⅓ nominal depth (Waal: 31 mm, Vecht: 26 mm) | TMS 402 corbelling rule, as summarised in BIA *Brick Brief: Corbels* (2025) and STRUCTURE magazine |
| Total corbel projection (relief) | ≤ ½ nominal depth from the face below (Waal: 55 mm). A wall's own lean or curve is checked course by course instead | same |
| Support under a brick | ≥ 40 % of its length rests on bricks below (or on a lintel / arch) | Hezarbaaf default; BIA recommends the first course bears on ≥ ⅔ of its thickness |
| Arch joints | 5 mm at the soffit to 19 mm at the back; outside that, tapered (gauged) voussoirs | BIA Technical Note 31: joints between arch bricks 3–19 mm |
| Arch ring depth | ≥ span / 12 and ≥ 100 mm; flat (jack) arches ≥ 100 mm + span / 12 and ≥ 200 mm | BIA Technical Note 31 |
| Smallest radius for standard bricks (towers) | (L + j)·D / (2(j − jmin)), the BIA curved-wall formula measured at the joint centre | BIA *Curved Brick Walls*: R = W (L/J + 1) |
| Bed-joint wire | courses where ≥ 60 % of head joints sit within ¼ brick of the joints below (stack bond), or ≥ 25 % of gaps are open (perforated screens): wire every 450 mm through that zone | TMS 402 prescriptive veneer rules (other than running bond needs joint reinforcement); STRUCTURE, "Sunlight and Shade" (perforated brickwork) |
| Turned bricks | head joint opened so neighbours can't touch; short cut pieces turn less | geometry |
| Recessed joints | a note: not for walls exposed to driving rain or frost; "deep" is 10 mm | BDA *Severely exposed brickwork*; manufacturer joint-profile guidance |

## Rod screens

Bricks on steel rods are carried by the rods, so the support and corbel rules don't apply. Hezarbaaf checks instead that
neighbours don't touch, that a brick on two rods isn't turned, and that no brick is pushed off its rod. Rod diameter,
spacing and the top and bottom fixings depend on wind load and are the engineer's job. Set the hole spacing from the
brick you will actually buy.

## Persian techniques

| Term | What the sources say | In Hezarbaaf |
|---|---|---|
| هزارباف (hezarbaaf) | relief brickwork with a woven look (Seljuk, 11th–12th c.) | Hezarbaaf Wall, motifs |
| بنّایی (banna'i) | glazed bricks alternating with plain bricks, often spelling sacred names | glaze letters G/T/N, Brick Tones |
| کوفی بنایی (square Kufic) | Kufic script laid with square bricks | «علی» motif; square brick format 200×200×45 |
| پنج‌وهفت (panj-o-haft) | the characteristic Persian four-centred pointed arch; base arc angles around 45–55° give the lowest thrust (Hayaty et al., 2025) | Arched Openings, K = 4, S = base angle |

Sources: BIA Technical Notes 31 and Brick Briefs (gobrick.com); STRUCTURE magazine; BDA; KNB (Dutch brick formats);
Encyclopaedia Iranica, "Brick"; Wikipedia, "Banna'i"; Shekofteh et al., Seljuk brickwork (SID); Hayaty, Shahriari & Moradi Nafchi (2025),
Journal of Fine Arts: Architecture & Urban Planning 30(3).
